# Litmus — Pitch Script

Rehearse this, not the code. Target: 90 seconds, walk-up-and-say-it easy. Four beats: problem, solution, demo, why us.

---

## The 90-second script

**Problem (15s).**
> "Every GTM team here pays for intent data. Half of it is wrong. False-positive rates run 31 to 47 percent, so reps burn days chasing accounts that were never in the market, and they torch their domain doing it. The whole industry sells you more signals. Nobody checks if they're true."

**Solution (15s).**
> "Litmus is the precision layer over your existing signals. You give it a signal. It gathers independent evidence, decides if it's real, and hands you the verdict with proof. It sits on top of any signal source and in front of any outreach tool."

**Demo (45s).** Three cards on screen. Point at each.
> "Three signals came in this morning. Signal one: a VP just moved to a company we sell to. Litmus checked, the person is real, the role is current, there's a second signal. Verdict: real. Go.
> Signal two looks just as hot. But watch. Litmus checked, and this person left that company four months ago. The signal is stale. Verdict: noise. Your rep just got a day back.
> Signal three: real person, real role, but only one weak signal. Verdict: needs a second signal. Don't waste a senior rep on it yet."

*(The middle card is the whole pitch. Slow down on it.)*

**Why us / why now (15s).**
> "The market's constraint moved. It's not volume anymore, it's precision. Litmus runs on Sillage's signals and FullEnrich's data, and it makes both of them provably actionable. We're not another signal source. We're the layer that tells you which ones to trust."

---

## The one framing rule (do not break)

When you talk about Sillage, the line is **"we make your signals provably actionable."**
Never **"your signals are unreliable"** or **"full of false positives."** Same fact, opposite reception. The false-positive stats are about *the market* (intent data broadly), never about Sillage. A Sillage judge should hear a partner, not a critic.

---

## The demo move that carries it

Make the middle card real and specific. Use a signal about a company or person the room actually recognizes, so "this person left four months ago, here's the proof" lands as true, not as a toy. The gotcha is the product. If it's flat, the pitch is flat. Rehearse the pause before the verdict.

---

## Judge Q&A prep

**"Isn't this just a feature Clay adds next week?"**
> "Clay orchestrates and enriches, it explicitly punts verification back to the operator. We're source-agnostic and sit above any tool, including Clay. The precision layer is a different job than the spreadsheet."

**"How is the verdict not just the model guessing?"**
> "Every verdict is rule-based and cited. Company unverifiable is noise. Person not in the role now is noise. Confirmed but uncorroborated is needs-a-second-signal. And it shows you the exact FullEnrich field behind each call. It's evidence, not vibes."

**"What's the accuracy?"**
> "We verify against reachable, current contact data, so we're checking ground truth, not scoring topic keywords. The honest answer is we kill the false positives you can prove are dead, and we flag the ones you can't confirm yet. That alone takes a rep's noise down hard."

**"Who uses this tomorrow?"**
> "Any team already paying for intent data and disappointed in the ROI. The reps who quietly stopped trusting the intent layer in month four. We turn it back on for them."

**"What did you build today vs before?"**
> "All of it today. The signal schema, the FullEnrich verification loop, the verdict logic, the evidence chain, live on real data."

---

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] One person pitches. One drives the demo. Do not narrate over each other.
- [ ] Screen shows the three cards before you start talking. No loading on stage.
- [ ] Demo runs from the pre-warm cache. One live call ready if a judge pushes.
- [ ] Rehearse three times. Time it. Cut anything past 90 seconds.
- [ ] The pause before the middle verdict is the most important half-second. Practice it.
